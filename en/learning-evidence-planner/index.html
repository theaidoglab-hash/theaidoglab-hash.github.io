What to inspect
Inspect the Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage functions. Choose one suggestion that fits a fictional scenario and identify the owner who must respond to one risk.

First-week artefact
Make a one-page risk-and-decision map: intended decision, allowed and prohibited input, affected people, measurement, owner, and one stop or response after a failure.

What it does not claim
Following a voluntary playbook does not establish compliance, approval, a completed risk assessment, or a safe launch.

Caveat
This framework can be tailored to context; it does not choose tool settings, legal responsibility, or a real business threshold for you.

What to inspect
See how a README can explain what a project does, how to start, where to get help, and who maintains it. Compare that with whether your work makes its decision and limit clear.

First-week artefact
Draft a local README: user decision, synthetic input, expected and negative case, how a reviewer can inspect it, limit, and source receipt.

What it does not claim
A README does not show that code is correct, that work is public, or that anyone reviewed it.

Caveat
A local README is enough for practice. Do not put employer material or unknown-source content in a public repository before ownership, licence, and privacy checks.

The smallest useful rehearsal

No-code: a reviewable draft workflow

Start with the supplied no-code lab below. It gives you the local materials; you do not need an AI account to practise the parts that make a draft safe to review.

Make this locally

  1. Write one fictional user, one decision a human keeps, one allowed input, one draft output, and one non-goal.
  2. Add two synthetic rows: one ordinary request and one missing, unsafe, or out-of-scope request.
  3. For each row, write the expected route (DRAFT, HANDOFF, or BLOCKED), then record the reviewer decision and one reason.

Someone can inspect

A plain input/output contract, two fixed cases, expected routes, and a visible exception path.

The work also shows

A named owner, a reason the final action stays human, and an honest limit on what the exercise demonstrates.

BEFORE YOU CALL IT A PORTFOLIO PROJECT

Carry the record forward; do not carry a success claim

This rehearsal can show that you kept a bounded decision, cases, and a review outcome. It cannot show business impact, model quality, production readiness, or professional experience. Move the evidence forward only after you can say exactly what was observed.

Keep these four pieces together

  • The fictional decision and the named person who keeps the final action.
  • The allowed synthetic input and the action that stays prohibited.
  • The six expected-versus-observed routes, including a handoff or blocked case.
  • The reviewer record and the reason for PASS, REVISE, or STOP.

REVISE means the record belongs in the next experiment, not a project claim

Keep the mismatch and make one narrower rerun. A visible revision can become useful evidence later; a completion claim cannot replace the missing case, boundary, or decision.
