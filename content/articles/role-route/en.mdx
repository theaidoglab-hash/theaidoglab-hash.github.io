> If one portfolio is trying to apply for four entirely different jobs, the problem is rarely that you built too little. It is that a reviewer cannot see whose decision you helped make.

## Read the deliverable before the title
When you read an AI or data role description, `AI Engineer`, `ML Engineer`, `Applied AI`, and even `Forward Deployed Engineer` are search entries—not job specifications. The same title can mean a model experiment, an internal-tool workflow, a dependable platform capability, or a jointly scoped and handed-over piece of work.

Do not begin by listing another framework. Read a job description as “what must be handed to whom each week, so that they can make which decision?” Circle the verbs in responsibility statements, then ask whether the deliverable could be rerun, reviewed, or rejected by somebody else.

| Common JD verbs | Likely primary deliverable | The minimum visible portfolio evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `train`, `experiment`, `benchmark` | A comparable model or data decision | baseline, split method, metric, error analysis |
| `build`, `integrate`, `ship` | An application flow a user will touch | input contract, tests, failure handling, handoff |
| `deploy`, `operate`, `secure`, `monitor` | A service capability that can be maintained | release gate, permissions, trace, rollback plan |
| `discover`, `scope`, `implement`, `adopt` | A customer or internal problem that is scoped and handed over | problem brief, trade-off, acceptance, owner |

These are reading aids, not a market taxonomy. A role can span two columns. Your task is to identify the deliverable most likely to be probed and one failure risk attached to it.

## Build an evidence map from three JDs
Choose three jobs you genuinely want and meet the basic eligibility conditions for. You do not need to put employer names, recruiter details, or whole adverts into a public notebook; retain the responsibility line and your own interpretation. For every responsibility, write four fields:

1. **What decision must the other person make?** For example: “The support lead must choose which cases receive human review first.”
2. **What evidence do I already have?** Not “I know Python,” but “I have a fixed test set and a failure taxonomy.”
3. **What is the largest missing link?** For example: a response is visible, but no permission boundary or queue capacity is shown.
4. **What could I retain in ten days?** A brief, a baseline, fixed cases, a reviewer decision, or a documented reason to stop.

When evaluation and deployment recur across all three JDs, do not open a fourth chatbot. Strengthen an existing demo into an inspectable evidence chain: fixed tests, failure categories, a data/action boundary, rerun steps, and a release or rollback decision. That is usually closer to the work being assessed than a more fashionable UI.

## Let the project follow the work risk
Imagine the responsibility statements are not chiefly “build RAG,” but “enable an internal team to use RAG safely.” The centre of the project should not be a polished answer screen. It could instead be a fully synthetic policy-support workflow that makes these things clear:

- which documents may be read and which data must not enter the flow;
- why a fast keyword baseline still exists;
- which answers must stop at `DRAFT` and go to a named reviewer;
- which fixed cases must block release; and
- who owns rollback or the next step after a failure.

That does not pretend you have operated a production system. It gives an interviewer concrete material to inspect: the system behaviour, the boundary around its use, and who takes the next decision. ## Replace an endless project with a ten-day contract
Write the next project as a short contract, not “build an AI app”:

- **Day 1:** Name the owner, decision, non-goal, and privacy boundary.
- **Days 2–4:** Create an explainable baseline and a small fixed set of synthetic cases.
- **Days 5–7:** Add one technical choice you intend to prove, such as retrieval, queue routing, or an evaluation gate.
- **Days 8–9:** Keep a failure record, trace, or reviewer decision instead of only recording a demo.
- **Day 10:** Write the README: how to rerun it, what it cannot claim, and the next decision a person must make.

If you still cannot write `pass`, `revise`, or `stop` after ten days, reduce the scope. The value of a portfolio is not its feature count; it is whether another person can understand how you made trade-offs.

## Some questions cannot be answered by a portfolio
A JD is not the whole job. Seniority, location, hiring eligibility, team maturity, and the real proportion of each responsibility still need separate checking. A portfolio cannot erase experience or eligibility requirements. Its role is to give someone concrete material to assess—not to guarantee an interview or an offer.
